Why us?

Integrating theory and practice

ERTHO draws on primary academic literature and original research to inform its theoretical understanding, while benefiting from practitioners' experience and knowledge that have put these concepts to the test in the real world.

An extended network

Just as an ecosystem consists of various organisms and communities interacting with one another and their environment, so individuals and organisations - each performing its specialised function - can interact productively with one another. ERTHO has access to a large and growing network of role-players and stakeholders in the Western Cape conservation stewardship sphere, and mediates between and connects these entities.

Private sector emphasis

We understand the pressures ordinary private landowners, communities, individuals and businesses are under with respect to environmental compliance, the socio-political context and economic factors. Though we involve all sectors - private, state and NGO - we champion the private sector as the building block of all others.

Conservancy Governance & Management Readiness Checklist

V1.1

Derived from: Ertho Conservancy Governance and Management Evaluation Matrix (V2, Aug 2026) and the author's underlying MPhil Environmental Management thesis best-practice text (Chapter 6)

**Purpose**

This checklist translates the normative conservancy model into a practical, sequenced set of tools, structures, management practices and governance elements a conservancy should put in place to move from inactive/nascent toward active and effective status.

**Structure**

Items are grouped by feature category (matching the evaluation matrix columns) and then by tier:

**Baseline**

The minimum elements needed for a conservancy to be considered genuinely ACTIVE (broadly equivalent to the 'slightly effective' threshold in the matrix). Without these, a conservancy risks being inactive or becoming defunct.

**Intermediate**

Elements that move a conservancy from merely active to 'moderately effective' — a functioning, reasonably resourced conservancy with regular (if not yet comprehensive) practice.

**Advanced**

Elements associated with 'highly effective' conservancies — mature governance, diversified partnerships, adaptive co-management and measurable environmental outcomes.
